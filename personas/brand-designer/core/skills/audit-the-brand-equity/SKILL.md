# Audit the brand equity — keep what's earned

People recognize a brand by a handful of assets they've seen repeatedly. Throwing those away resets
recognition to zero; keeping a weak one by inertia caps the brand forever. Decide on evidence.

## The inventory
Walk every surface the product has (site, README, docs, CLI output, package page, social profiles, slides,
the product UI) and list each asset:
- **Name and its written form** (case, spacing, punctuation, any prefix like `>` or `$`).
- **Mark / wordmark**, favicon, avatar.
- **Colours** actually in use (pull the hex values from the code, not memory).
- **Type** in use and its licence.
- **Motifs and devices** (a terminal prompt, a shape, a pattern, an illustration style).
- **Phrases** people repeat — taglines, section headings, the founder's own lines.
- **Tone** — how it talks today, with two verbatim examples.

## Rate each asset
| Asset | Recognition (how often seen, how consistent) | Fit with the brand idea | Uniqueness in category | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
Decisions: **keep** (strong on all three), **evolve** (recognized but mis-fitting — keep the memory,
redraw the form), **retire** (weak or generic) — each with a one-line reason the founder can check.

## The discipline
- A young product with little exposure has little equity — say so, and feel free to move further.
- Founder-tuned lines and assets are equity until proven otherwise; replace only with something
  demonstrably better, and show both.
- Put the audit at the top of the brand deliverable; every later choice should trace to it.
