# Forecast from drivers — numbers that survive a domain expert

A forecast that a practitioner laughs at ("thirteen big companies are fighting for it and you say it stays
tiny?") is worse than no forecast. The usual failure is extrapolating one historical analogue in a
straight line and calling a timid case "optimistic". Build the number from *why* it would move.

## The loop
1. **List the drivers before the numbers.** What actually moves volume here: distribution channels and who
   pushes them, incentives (bundling, promotions, speculation), competitive intensity and what it signals,
   recent velocity, regulatory or structural changes, and demand with no historical precedent. Ask the
   founder what they see that the data doesn't — they often hold the decisive driver.
2. **Pick analogues — and say where each breaks.** "Like X in year Y, except there was no Z then." An
   analogue with no stated break is a guess with a citation.
3. **Build the cases from the drivers.** Base = drivers at their observed strength. Optimistic = every
   lever works *and sticks* (not "promotions fade"). Pessimistic = named levers fail. No flat-lined years
   unless a driver says so. Year-by-year, not a single endpoint, when the shape matters.
4. **Sanity-check every case.** Against real comparables (the closest 3–5, with their actual numbers), hard
   bounds (how many could exist at all?), and the domain's own definitions. If a case lands outside the
   comparables, explain which driver justifies it — or fix it.
5. **Show the work briefly.** A small table: driver → assumption → effect, the three cases, the comparables
   beside them. Name the one assumption that, if wrong, moves the answer most.

## The critique pass
- Would someone who works in this market call any case naive? Which driver would they say is missing?
- Is "optimistic" actually optimistic? Is "base" what you'd bet on?
- Does every number trace to a source or a stated assumption?
- Did the founder's stated facts and drivers make it in, verbatim where given?

Pairs with `weight-the-evidence` (how strong each input is) and `triangulate` (independent sources).
