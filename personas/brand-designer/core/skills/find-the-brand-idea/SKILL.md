# Find the brand idea — one sentence everything traces to

A mood board is not a strategy. Great identities start from an **idea** — usually a truth about what the
product *does* or *believes*, expressed so it can be seen and heard. "Momentum, not automation" (Windsurf);
"the product development system" set as rigour and speed (Linear). The idea is what makes the system
coherent and what lets the next designer extend it.

## Inputs (read, don't invent)
- The positioning from the **product-marketer**: who it's for, the alternative they use, what's different,
  the proof. If there's none, get it before designing — don't make up a market.
- The product itself: what it literally does, its mechanics, its artifacts, the words in its code.
- The equity audit (`audit-the-brand-equity`).
- The category map (`test-distinctiveness`).

## Produce
1. **The idea** — one sentence. Test: could a named competitor say it truthfully? Then it's not the idea.
2. **Where it comes from** — the product truth it rests on, in a line.
3. **Personality** — 3–4 traits, each with "…not …" (e.g. *precise, not cold*; *expert, not exclusive*).
4. **Design principles** — 3–5 rules that turn the idea into choices ("type does the work, decoration
   doesn't"; "every graphic is a real artifact of the product").
5. **Territories** — 4–6 different ways to express the idea visually, each one paragraph + a rendered
   sketch on a real touchpoint. Recommend one, say why the others lost.

## The discipline
- If the idea needs a paragraph to explain, it isn't one yet.
- The idea is a tool for deciding, not a tagline; it may never appear on the page.
- Bring the territories with your recommendation; the founder ratifies the direction.
