# Test distinctiveness — own a territory, don't join a crowd

Recognition comes from **distinctive** assets (Romaniuk): ones people link to *you* and nobody else. Most
categories converge — dev tools on black + gradient + geometric sans; AI tools on purple glows and sparkles.
Joining the convention makes you invisible.

## Map the category
Screenshot the homepage, favicon and social card of every notable competitor and adjacent tool (search for
the current set — not a remembered list) — **and the platform the product is built on or sold through**
(a third-party tool must not look first-party). Record for each: dominant colour(s), mark type (letter, symbol,
wordmark), typeface style, imagery style, tone. Lay them on one board. Name the conventions (what ≥half do)
and the empty territories.

## The tests (run on your direction, rendered)
- **Logo swap:** put a competitor's name in your layout. If it still looks right, you have no identity yet.
- **Squint / blur:** at 10% size or blurred, is it still recognizably yours (shape, colour, rhythm)?
- **Silhouette:** filled solid black, is the mark still identifiable?
- **One colour + reversed:** does it survive without its colour, and on dark and light?
- **Lineup:** your favicon among ten real browser tabs; your social card among a real feed — does it pop
  without shouting?

## The discipline
- Break a category convention only on purpose, and keep the ones that carry meaning users rely on.
- Distinctive is not loud: an owned colour used consistently beats a louder one used everywhere.
- Record the board and the test results in the deliverable — that's the evidence for the direction.
