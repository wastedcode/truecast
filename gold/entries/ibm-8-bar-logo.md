# IBM 8-bar logo — Paul Rand, 1972

**Why it's gold**
- One device carries the whole identity: the letters set in eight (sometimes 13) horizontal bars.
- Works in a single color; IBM's own history page shows it in black, gray, white or IBM Blue.
- Part of a lineage Rand managed: he drew the 1956 version, the 1972 bars and the 1981 rebus poster, so the
  mark evolved instead of being replaced.
- Fifty-plus years in use with only color and context adjusted.

**Weakness**
- Fine bars thin out at small sizes; check a 16px favicon version separately.
- The page is a history, not a construction spec: no grid, clear space or minimum size.

**Use when** an established company wants a typographic mark that must survive one-color reproduction.
